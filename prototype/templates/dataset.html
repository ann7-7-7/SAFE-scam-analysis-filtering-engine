Scam Types & Safety Guide

Learn how common scams work, what warning signs to look for, and how to stay safe before clicking, replying or sending money.

Examples are synthetic and used for awareness and testing. Do not paste real personal or sensitive information into the checker.

Crypto / Investment Scam

Scammers promise fast returns and guaranteed profits to push risky transfers.

  • Guaranteed return language
  • Urgent “limited slot” claims
  • Unknown trading links
  • Pressure to send crypto first

Safety advice: Never invest based on unsolicited messages. Verify through FCA-regulated sources.

Device Compromise / Extortion Scam

Scammers claim that your phone, account or device has been hacked or infected. They may demand money, pressure you to download software, or threaten that your data will be leaked or your device will be blocked.

  • Claims your phone or device is hacked
  • Demands payment to fix or protect the device
  • Asks you to download or install unknown software
  • Uses fear about leaked data, blocked accounts or malware
  • Gives urgent instructions from an unknown sender

Safety advice: Do not send money or download unknown files. Use trusted antivirus or official device support channels if you are concerned.

Why scammers move conversations to private messaging apps

Platforms such as WhatsApp, Telegram and social media DMs are not scams themselves, but scammers may misuse them because fake profiles, deleted messages, anonymous usernames and group pressure can make verification harder.

  • Being moved quickly to Telegram, WhatsApp or another private messaging app
  • being added to investment or task groups
  • being asked for crypto, gift cards or bank transfers
  • Deleted messages, changing usernames or anonymous profiles
  • pressure not to speak to friends or family

Common red flags

Urgency

Creates panic so you act before checking.

Suspicious links

Lookalike domains or shortened URLs hide fake pages.

Money requests

Unexpected payments, transfer fees, or release charges.

OTP/password requests

Legitimate services do not ask for OTPs, passwords or PINs through unsolicited messages.

Impersonation

Scammers pretend to be banks, delivery firms, or government.

Unrealistic rewards

“Guaranteed profit” or huge pay for minimal effort.

Emotional pressure

Uses fear, guilt, or family emergency stories.

Requests to move platform

Moves chats to private apps where verification is harder.
